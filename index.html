Everything's Not O.K.

NHL Concussions, Head Trauma and Player Safety

Inspired by Nick Boynton's first-person account in The Players' Tribune, this project explores the long-term effects of concussions in professional hockey and examines how the NHL has responded with new player safety measures.

Concussion Rates by Sport

Hockey is consistently ranked among the highest-risk sports for concussions. Comparing concussion rates across sports helps place Nick Boynton’s experience in a broader context and demonstrates that head trauma remains a major issue for athletes competing in contact sports.

Source: NCAA Concussion Surveillance Research

The NHL Increased Concussion Protections Over Time

As awareness of brain injuries has grown, the NHL has implemented additional safeguards for players. New concussion evaluations, baseline testing, league spotters, and return-to-play requirements were developed in response to mounting concerns about long-term neurological health.

Source: NHL and NHLPA Concussion Protocol Documentation

Head Trauma Is a League-Wide Issue Across the NHL

Concussion concerns affect players throughout the National Hockey League. This map shows all NHL franchises across the United States and Canada, illustrating the geographic scope of a player-safety issue that extends across the league.

Source: National Hockey League Team Location Data

Why This Matters

Nick Boynton's story underscores the lasting consequences of repeated head injuries. While the NHL has taken steps to improve player safety, concussions continue to be one of the most significant health concerns facing professional hockey players. Understanding the data behind these injuries provides important context for the physical and mental health challenges described in "Everything’s Not O.K."
